Argument & Disagreement

The product team was split. Half wanted to launch now. Half wanted to wait.

Maya wasn't interested in hearing both sides. She banged the table and listed every reason waiting was a disaster. The room grew tense. People stopped making eye contact.

Daniel sighed. He raised his palms. "Let's not fight about this — we all want the same thing." He started finding points everyone could agree on, softening the edges.

Priya had listened the whole time. Finally, she spoke: "We are not launching next week. That is final." No hedging. No room for debate. Just the decision, plain.

Maya picked a fight. Daniel smoothed it over. Priya closed it.

📖 Words

Word 1 of 3
contentious
/kənˈtɛn.ʃəs/ · kən-TEN-shəs · adjective · 有爭議的，引起爭論的
Likely to cause disagreement or argument. Can describe both a person (argumentative) and a topic (controversial).
↳ Back to the story: Maya was contentious — she escalated the meeting instead of finding common ground.
📰 News:The new housing policy proved highly contentious, sparking protests on both sides.
💼 Work:His contentious management style alienated half the team within a month.
🏠 Personal:Money is always a contentious topic at family dinners.
Word 2 of 3
conciliatory
/kənˈsɪl.i.ə.tər.i/ · kən-SIL-ee-ə-tər-ee · adjective · 安撫的，調和的
Intended to make peace, reduce tension, or resolve a disagreement. A conciliatory gesture says "I want us to get along."
↳ Back to the story: Daniel was conciliatory — he softened the conflict instead of fuelling it.
🌍 Diplomacy:The president struck a conciliatory tone in his speech, offering to negotiate.
💼 Work:She sent a conciliatory email after the debate — "Let's find a way forward together."
👫 Personal:His conciliatory apology — a quiet "I'm sorry" over coffee — diffused the tension.
Word 3 of 3
unequivocal
/ˌʌn.ɪˈkwɪv.ə.kəl/ · un-ih-KWIV-ə-kəl · adjective · 明確的，毫不含糊的
Leaving no doubt; completely clear and unambiguous. An unequivocal statement has no "maybe" or "I think" — it is a firm position.
↳ Back to the story: Priya was unequivocal — "We are not launching next week. That is final." No grey area.
⚖️ Law:The DNA evidence was unequivocal — the suspect was at the scene.
🔬 Science:Decades of research show an unequivocal link between smoking and lung cancer.
💼 Work:I need an unequivocal answer by Friday — yes or no.

💬 Phrases

Phrase 1 of 2
see eye to eye
To agree fully with someone. Most often used in the negative — "we don't see eye to eye" means we fundamentally disagree on something.
· 看法一致，意見相同
↳ Back to the story: Maya and Daniel didn't see eye to eye — she wanted confrontation, he wanted harmony.
👫 Personal:My brother and I don't see eye to eye on politics, but we respect each other's choices.
💼 Work:The design and engineering teams rarely see eye to eye on deadlines — one wants polish, the other wants delivery.
Phrase 2 of 2
play devil's advocate
To argue an opposing position for the sake of debate — not because you actually believe it, but to test the strength of the other side's reasoning.
· 故意唱反調，扮演反面角色
↳ Back to the story: Someone should play devil's advocate and argue why launching next week actually makes sense — just to test the team's conviction.
💼 Work:Let me play devil's advocate for a moment — what if the data is wrong and we make the wrong call?
🎓 Academic:The professor asked a student to play devil's advocate in the debate to strengthen both sides of the argument.
