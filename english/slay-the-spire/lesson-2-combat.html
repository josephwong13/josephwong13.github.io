Into Battle

The enemy's intent flashes above its head: attacking for 18 damage. You have three energy and a hand of five cards. Time to think.

You play a card that makes the enemy vulnerable — suddenly it takes 50% more damage from your attacks. Another card that gives you Block, shielding you from harm. Then you unleash a potent strike, dealing massive damage in a single blow. The enemy staggers.

But the fight isn't over. More enemies surround you. Their attacks could overwhelm you if you're not careful. You must hold your ground — block what you can, strike when it's safe, and try to weather the storm until the moment is right.

Combat in the Spire is a puzzle. Every turn, you choose: defend, attack, or set up for a bigger play next round.

📖 Words

Word 1 of 3
vulnerable
/ˈvʌl.nər.ə.bəl/ · VUL-nər-ə-bəl · adjective · 脆弱的；易受傷害的
Exposed to the possibility of being attacked or harmed, either physically or emotionally. In the game, applying Vulnerable to an enemy makes them take 50% more damage from attacks.
↳ From the story: You play a card that makes the enemy vulnerable — suddenly it takes 50% more damage.
💻 Tech:Outdated software leaves your system vulnerable to security breaches and ransomware attacks.
❤️ Personal:Opening up about your fears makes you feel vulnerable, but it's the only way to build real trust.
🏢 Business:Startups are especially vulnerable in their first year — one bad quarter can end everything.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • vulnerable (adj., base form) — exposed to harm. The injured animal was vulnerable to predators.
  • vulnerability (n.) — the state of being exposed; also a specific weakness. The report listed several security vulnerabilities.
  • vulnerabilities (n., plural) — multiple weaknesses. Three vulnerabilities were patched in the latest update.
  • vulnerably (adv.) — in an exposed manner (rare in everyday use). She smiled vulnerably, unsure how he would respond.
  • vulnerable to + noun — most common grammar: vulnerable to attack, vulnerable to criticism, vulnerable to disease. Elderly patients are especially vulnerable to the flu.
  • in a vulnerable position / state — common phrasing. The company is in a vulnerable position after the data leak.
  • invulnerable (adj., opposite) — impossible to harm. He seemed invulnerable to criticism.
  • leave someone/something vulnerable — common verb pattern. The layoffs left the team vulnerable to burnout.
Word 2 of 3
overwhelm
/ˌəʊ.vəˈwɛlm/ · oh-vər-WELM · verb · 壓倒；淹沒；深深打動
To defeat or overcome completely; to give too much of something so they cannot cope. Can also mean deeply moved emotionally (a positive sense).
↳ From the story: More enemies surround you — their attacks could overwhelm you if you're not careful.
💼 Work:She was overwhelmed by the flood of emails after returning from a two-week vacation.
🎓 Learning:Don't try to learn everything at once — you'll overwhelm yourself and retain nothing.
🥲 Emotion:He was overwhelmed with gratitude when his colleagues threw him a surprise farewell party.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • overwhelm (v., base form) — flood, defeat, or deeply move. The crowd began to overwhelm the security line.
  • overwhelmed (adj. / past participle) — flooded and unable to cope; or deeply moved emotionally. She felt overwhelmed by the kindness she received.
  • overwhelming (adj.) — extremely strong, intense, or large. The vote was an overwhelming majority — 90% in favor.
  • overwhelmingly (adv.) — to an extreme degree. The audience responded overwhelmingly positively to the new product.
  • overwhelm + with + noun — common grammar: overwhelm someone with work, overwhelm them with emotion, overwhelm with questions. The reporters overwhelmed the spokesperson with questions.
  • overwhelmed by + noun — passive form: feeling flooded by something. He was overwhelmed by grief.
  • overwhelming majority / victory / success — very common collocations. The candidate won by an overwhelming margin.
  • underwhelm (v., opposite) — to fail to impress. The sequel underwhelmed critics and audiences alike.
Word 3 of 3
potent
/ˈpəʊ.tənt/ · POH-tənt · adjective · 強效的；有力的
Having great power, influence, or effect. A potent attack deals massive damage; a potent argument wins debates instantly.
↳ From the story: You unleash a potent strike, dealing massive damage in a single blow.
💊 Medicine:This is a potent antibiotic — one dose is usually enough to clear the infection.
💼 Work:Her potent combination of technical skill and people management made her the obvious choice for team lead.
📝 Writing:A potent opening sentence can grab the reader's attention and never let go.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • potent (adj., base form) — very strong or effective. The new drug is extremely potent.
  • potency (n.) — the strength or effectiveness of something. The caffeine in this coffee has serious potency.
  • potently (adv.) — in a powerful, effective way. The argument potently reframed the entire debate.
  • impotent (adj., opposite) — powerless or unable to act. The new law proved impotent against the well-funded lobby.
  • potent + noun — common collocations: potent weapon, potent force, potent symbol, potent reminder. The portrait became a potent symbol of the era.
  • potent against + noun — used especially for drugs/chemicals. The remedy is potent against most strains of the virus.
  • with potent effect — common adverb phrase. His words landed with potent effect on the audience.
  • potency vs. power — note: potency often implies a hidden or concentrated strength (a drug, an argument), while power is broader. The potion's potency was greater than expected.

💬 Phrases

Phrase 1 of 2
hold your ground
To refuse to retreat or give in; to defend your position firmly under pressure.
· 堅守陣地；寸步不讓
↳ From the story: You must hold your ground — block what you can, and strike when it's safe.
💼 Work:When the client demanded an impossible deadline, she held her ground and refused to promise what the team couldn't deliver.
💬 Debate:The politician held his ground during the heated interview, refusing to be baited into a soundbite.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • hold your ground — base form. Hold your ground until reinforcements arrive.
  • held (one's) ground (past) — already defended the position. She held her ground against the chairman's pressure.
  • holding (one's) ground (continuous) — currently defending. He's still holding his ground on the price.
  • stand (one's) ground — same meaning, common variant. The villagers stood their ground against the raiders.
  • hold your ground on + noun — common grammar: hold your ground on the price, on the principle. He held his ground on every clause.
  • dig in (your heels) — close synonym: to become stubborn in a position. She dug in her heels and refused to apologize.
  • give ground (opposite) — to retreat or concede. The union refused to give ground on wages.
  • hold the line — related: maintain the current position against pressure. Hold the line until the cavalry arrives.
Phrase 2 of 2
weather the storm
To survive a difficult or unpleasant situation without being permanently harmed; to endure a period of hardship until things improve.
· 渡過難關；熬過風暴
↳ From the story: You must weather the storm — survive this round of damage and the fight is yours.
🏢 Business:The airline managed to weather the storm of the pandemic by cutting costs early and raising cash reserves.
💼 Work:After two rounds of layoffs, the remaining team weathered the storm and rebuilt morale over the next year.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • weather the storm — base form of the idiom. We'll weather the storm together.
  • weathered the storm (past) — already survived. She weathered the storm of public criticism with dignity.
  • weathering the storm (continuous) — currently enduring. The economy is weathering the storm better than expected.
  • weather + noun — note: "weather" can also be used with other nouns (weather a crisis, weather a scandal). The government weathered a political crisis last year.
  • ride out the storm — close synonym, slightly more about waiting it out. We'll ride out the storm and reassess next quarter.
  • weather the storm of + noun — common grammar: storm of criticism, storm of controversy. He weathered the storm of accusations and stayed in office.
  • before / after the storm — common paired phrases. Things look bleak now, but after the storm we'll recover.
  • make it through / pull through — non-idiom alternatives meaning the same thing. We pulled through the recession with no layoffs.
