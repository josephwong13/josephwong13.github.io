The Ascent

You wake at the base of the Spire. A mysterious whale, Neow, offers you a gift — an ancient relic to aid your climb. You have nothing but a basic deck of strikes and defends.

Every floor you ascend tests your resolve. You battle Cultists, Sentries, and the occasional elite — each victory rewards you with new cards, gold, or potions. You learn to forge your deck carefully, choosing only the cards that strengthen your strategy.

At campfires, you face a choice: rest to recover lost health, or forge a card at the smith to make it permanently stronger — a calculated risk. Do you start from scratch, building something precise, or do you take every reward offered?

Whatever you choose, you must forge ahead. The Spire waits for no one.

📖 Words

Word 1 of 3
ascend
/əˈsɛnd/ · ə-SEND · verb · 攀登；上升
To climb or move upward; to rise to a higher level or rank. In the game, each successful run unlocks higher Ascension levels.
↳ From the story: Every floor you ascend tests your resolve — the higher you climb, the greater the challenge.
💼 Work:She ascended through the ranks over a decade to become the company's first female CEO.
🏔️ Travel:The climbers ascended the final 500 meters at dawn, reaching the summit as the sun rose.
📈 Growth:With each successful project, his reputation ascended to new heights in the industry.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • ascend (v., base form) — move upward. The plane ascended through the clouds.
  • ascended (v., past tense) — finished climbing or rising. He had ascended to the top of the mountain by noon.
  • ascending (v., present participle / adj.) — currently moving upward; or describing something that goes up. The road was an ascending switchback.
  • ascension (n.) — the act of rising; also the in-game difficulty mode. She completed her first A20 ascension last week.
  • ascendant (n.) — a position of growing power or influence ("in the ascendant"). The new technology is in the ascendant — replacing the old within five years.
  • descend (v., opposite) — to move or go downward. The plane began to descend for landing.
  • ascend to + noun — common collocation: ascend to power, ascend to the throne, ascend to fame. She ascended to fame with her debut novel.
  • ascend the throne — fixed phrase meaning to become king/queen. The prince ascended the throne at just nineteen.
Word 2 of 3
relic
/ˈrɛl.ɪk/ · REL-ik · noun · 遺物；聖物
An object surviving from an earlier time, especially one of historical or sentimental value. Relics in Slay the Spire provide powerful passive bonuses throughout your run.
↳ From the story: Neow gifted you an ancient relic — a small charm that would grow stronger with every battle won.
🏛️ History:The museum's newest exhibit features relics from the Ming Dynasty recovered from a shipwreck.
🎮 Gaming:That family board game has become a cherished relic — its pieces worn smooth by decades of play.
💬 Personal:My grandfather's pocket watch is the only relic I have left of him.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • relic (n., base form) — an old surviving object. The sword was a relic from the Bronze Age.
  • relics (n., plural) — multiple such objects; also used in many StS card names. The boss dropped three relics at once.
  • relic of + noun — common pattern: relic of the past, relic of a bygone era. This law is a relic of the 19th century.
  • relict (adj., rare/scientific) — surviving from the past; used in biology. A relict species found only on this remote island.
  • relic-like (adj.) — resembling an old artifact. The abandoned factory had a relic-like quality.
  • living relic — a fixed phrase for something old that still exists in the present. That cafe is a living relic of the 1950s.
  • relic hunter / relic hunter (n.) — a person who searches for old artifacts. The documentary follows relic hunters in the Andes.
Word 3 of 3
forge
/fɔːdʒ/ · FORJ · verb / noun · 鍛造；打造；開闢
To shape or create something with great effort — originally metal by heating and hammering, but now broadly: to build, to establish, or to move ahead with determination.
↳ From the story: At the campfire, you could rest — or forge a card at the smith, making it permanently stronger.
🤝 Relationships:They forged a deep friendship during those long nights working on the startup together.
💼 Work:The two companies forged a partnership that reshaped the entire industry.
🛤️ Life:She left her hometown at eighteen and forged her own path, against everyone's advice.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • forge (v., base form) — create or shape, often through effort. He forged a knife from a railroad spike.
  • forged (v., past tense) — already created; also = faked (a forged signature, a forged painting). The will was forged, not signed by the deceased.
  • forging (v., present participle / gerund) — currently shaping or creating. She is forging a new career in design.
  • forge (n.) — a workshop with a furnace for shaping metal. The blacksmith worked the bellows at the village forge.
  • forger (n.) — a person who fakes documents, signatures, or artworks. The painting was exposed as the work of a master forger.
  • forge + noun — common patterns: forge a bond, forge an alliance, forge a career, forge a path. They forged an alliance that lasted decades.
  • forge ahead — phrasal verb meaning to keep moving forward with determination (covered as a separate phrase below).
  • forgery (n.) — a fake copy of a document, signature, or artwork. The museum discovered the painting was a clever forgery.

💬 Phrases

Phrase 1 of 2
start from scratch
To begin again from the very beginning, with nothing prepared or carried over.
· 從零開始，從頭再來
↳ From the story: Every run starts from scratch — only your knowledge carries over from your last attempt.
💼 Work:After the acquisition fell through, we had to start from scratch on the entire proposal.
🍳 Daily life:I burned the cake so badly I had to start from scratch — new batter, new pan, everything.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • start from scratch — base form of the idiom. Let's start from scratch tomorrow.
  • started from scratch (past) — already happened. He started from scratch and built a million-dollar business in five years.
  • starting from scratch (continuous) — ongoing action. We're starting from scratch on the design.
  • build from scratch — close variant: also means to create from nothing. The framework was built from scratch in two weeks.
  • cook from scratch — common collocation: cook a meal using raw ingredients, not pre-made. She cooks everything from scratch — no shortcuts.
  • from scratch — the phrase is also used on its own as shorthand, without "start" or "build". She cooked the whole meal from scratch.
  • start from a clean slate — a synonym idiom, common in work/school contexts. After the scandal, the CEO wanted to start from a clean slate.
Phrase 2 of 2
forge ahead
To continue moving forward with determination, especially when progress is difficult.
· 奮勇前進，毅然前行
↳ From the story: Whatever you choose, you must forge ahead — the Spire waits for no one.
💼 Work:Despite losing their biggest client, the team decided to forge ahead with the new product launch.
🏃 Sport:Exhausted but unwilling to quit, the marathon runner forged ahead through the final kilometers.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • forge ahead — base form. Let's forge ahead with the plan.
  • forged ahead (past) — already pushed forward. She forged ahead despite the criticism.
  • forging ahead (continuous) — currently pushing forward. The startup is forging ahead with its Series B.
  • forge ahead with + noun — common pattern, where the noun is what's being pushed. They forged ahead with the merger talks.
  • forge on — close variant with the same meaning. Tired but determined, she forged on through the night.
  • press on / push on — synonym phrases meaning to keep going. Press on — we're almost at the summit.
  • forge ahead despite + noun — typical grammar: advance even when facing difficulty. She forged ahead despite the mounting losses.
  • press forward / move forward — non-idiom equivalents that mean the same thing. The team pressed forward against the deadline.
