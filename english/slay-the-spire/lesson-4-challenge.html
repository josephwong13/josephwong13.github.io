The Gauntlet

You've beaten the Spire. Now the real test begins — Ascension mode. Twenty levels of escalating difficulty. Each one adds a cumulative penalty: enemies hit harder, elites appear more often, and healing is scarcer.

What was once a challenging climb becomes grueling. At Ascension 17, normal enemies gain new, deadlier move sets. At Ascension 20, you face two consecutive Act 3 bosses. The Spire has become a gauntlet — unforgiving, punishing every mistake with no second chances.

Every player who reaches the top has learned to rise to the challenge. They've died hundreds of times and still pushed through. Because in the Spire, the only way out is up.

📖 Words

Word 1 of 3
grueling
/ˈɡruː.ə.lɪŋ/ · GROO-ə-ling · adjective · 極度疲勞的；艱苦的
Extremely tiring and demanding; punishing in its duration or intensity. A grueling challenge tests your limits.
↳ From the story: What was once challenging becomes grueling — the Spire at high Ascension drains everything you have.
🏃 Sport:The Ironman triathlon is a grueling test of endurance — 3.8 km of swimming, 180 km of cycling, then a full marathon.
💼 Work:After a grueling 80-hour week to meet the deadline, the team was given a long weekend to recover.
🎓 Education:The final-year medical exams were grueling — three days of back-to-back written and practical tests.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • grueling (adj., base form) — extremely tiring. The hike was grueling. (Also spelled gruelling in British English.)
  • gruelingly (adv.) — in an exhausting manner. The course was gruelingly difficult.
  • grueling + noun — common collocations: grueling schedule, grueling journey, grueling exam, grueling pace. The athlete kept a grueling training schedule.
  • a grueling + noun — often used as a noun phrase: a grueling contest, a grueling ordeal. The rescue was a grueling 18-hour operation.
  • gruel (n.) — the root noun: a thin watery porridge. Historically, "grueling" implied punishment as harsh as being forced to eat gruel.
  • exhausting / punishing / arduous — close synonyms, but grueling specifically emphasizes physical or mental exhaustion over a long stretch. The exam was exhausting, but the marathon was truly grueling.
  • brutal (related) — even stronger than grueling; emphasizes harshness as well as duration. The winter conditions were brutal.
  • ease / rest (opposite ideas) — the antonym is usually expressed as a contrasting noun (an easy/a relaxing experience), not a single word.
Word 2 of 3
cumulative
/ˈkjuː.mjʊ.lə.tɪv/ · KYOO-myoo-lə-tiv · adjective · 累積的；漸增的
Increasing in effect, quantity, or strength by successive additions. Each Ascension level adds a cumulative effect — they stack and grow harder together.
↳ From the story: Each Ascension level adds a cumulative penalty — they stack, making each new tier harder than the last.
📊 Science:The cumulative effect of sleep deprivation over several days is more dangerous than most people realize.
💰 Finance:Thanks to cumulative interest, even small monthly investments can grow into a substantial sum over decades.
🏋️ Training:Muscle growth is cumulative — you won't see results after one workout, but over months the change is dramatic.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • cumulative (adj., base form) — building up by successive additions. The cumulative damage was fatal.
  • cumulatively (adv.) — in a way that builds up. The errors cumulatively caused the outage.
  • accumulate (v.) — the base verb: to build up over time. Dust had accumulated on the shelves.
  • accumulation (n.) — the build-up itself. An accumulation of small mistakes led to the disaster.
  • cumulative + noun — common collocations: cumulative effect, cumulative damage, cumulative impact, cumulative GPA. The cumulative impact of years of pollution is showing up now.
  • accumulator (n.) — something that collects or builds up (also a type of betting). The battery is an accumulator of electrical charge.
  • build up / pile up (synonyms) — verbs with the same meaning. Stress built up over the year.
  • vs. instantaneous — note: cumulative emphasizes time and addition; the opposite is something that happens all at once.
Word 3 of 3
unforgiving
/ˌʌn.fəˈɡɪv.ɪŋ/ · un-fər-GIV-ing · adjective · 不寬容的；嚴苛的
Not willing to forgive mistakes or grant second chances; harsh in the consequences it imposes. An unforgiving system punishes every error with no mercy.
↳ From the story: The Spire becomes unforgiving — one misplay and your run is over, no take-backs.
🌊 Nature:The unforgiving mountain climate killed two of the three climbers within hours of the storm.
💻 Tech:The compiler is famously unforgiving — a single missing semicolon and the whole build fails.
💼 Work:The first 90 days at a new job are unforgiving; small mistakes leave a lasting impression.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • unforgiving (adj., base form) — harsh, no second chances. The desert is unforgiving at midday.
  • forgive (v.) — the base verb; to pardon. Please forgive me for being late.
  • forgave (v., past) — already pardoned. She forgave him years ago.
  • forgiven (v., past participle) — having been pardoned. All sins are forgiven.
  • forgiving (adj., opposite) — willing to overlook mistakes. Try a forgiving camera mode for beginners.
  • forgiveness (n.) — the act of pardoning. She asked for forgiveness.
  • unforgiveness (n., rare) — the state of being unwilling to pardon. Her unforgiveness poisoned the whole family.
  • unforgiving of + noun — common grammar: unforgiving of mistakes, unforgiving of failure. The industry is unforgiving of newcomers who don't adapt.

💬 Phrases

Phrase 1 of 2
rise to the challenge
To respond with determination and ability when faced with a difficult situation; to prove yourself equal to the task.
· 迎接挑戰；挺身而出
↳ From the story: Every player who reaches the top has learned to rise to the challenge, no matter how impossible it seemed.
💼 Work:When the team lead quit mid-project, the junior developer rose to the challenge and delivered the release on time.
🎓 Education:She rose to the challenge of learning a new language in six months, practicing for hours every single day.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • rise to the challenge — base form. It's time to rise to the challenge.
  • rose to the challenge (past) — already met it. She rose to the challenge and led the team through the crisis.
  • rising to the challenge (continuous) — currently meeting it. The company is rising to the challenge of remote work.
  • rise to the occasion — common variant, same meaning. He rose to the occasion when it mattered most.
  • rise to + noun — broader pattern: rise to fame, rise to power, rise to prominence. She rose to fame in her twenties.
  • step up — synonym: to take on a bigger role. When things got hard, she stepped up.
  • rise above + noun — related: to overcome something difficult. He rose above the criticism and kept going.
  • meet the challenge head-on — alternative idiom with the same meaning. She met the challenge head-on.
Phrase 2 of 2
push through
To continue making progress despite difficulty, exhaustion, or resistance; to persevere until you break through.
· 堅持到底；突破難關
↳ From the story: They died hundreds of times and still pushed through — because the only way out is up.
🏃 Fitness:When your legs are burning and you want to stop — that's the moment you push through and find what you're really made of.
💼 Work:The legislation faced fierce opposition, but the committee pushed through and passed it by a single vote.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • push through — base form. Let's push through to the finish line.
  • pushed through (past) — already forced past. She pushed through the pain barrier.
  • pushing through (continuous) — currently forcing past. We're pushing through the final stretch.
  • push through + noun — common pattern, with the noun being what's forced past. The bill was pushed through parliament by a single vote.
  • push through with + noun — also common. She pushed through with the plan despite the doubts.
  • push on / press on — synonyms: keep going. Push on — we're almost there.
  • pull through — close synonym meaning to survive a difficult period (often health). He pulled through the surgery.
  • give up / throw in the towel (opposite) — to stop trying. She wanted to give up, but pushed through.
