The Spire's Secrets

Hidden throughout the Spire are truths most climbers never discover. An obscure event requires you to sacrifice a relic to unlock a secret. Three keys scattered across your run open a door to Act IV — the true heart of the Spire.

Players spent years trying to unravel the Spire's deepest secrets. Why does Neow send us up the tower? What is the Corruption that consumes everything? The answers are pivotal to understanding the game's true meaning.

You must piece together clues from relics and events, and against all odds, face the Corrupted Heart itself — a battle few ever win.

📖 Words

Word 1 of 3
unravel
/ʌnˈræv.əl/ · un-RAV-əl · verb · 解開；揭開（謎團）；拆散
To untangle or solve something complex; to investigate and explain a mystery. Also: to come apart or disintegrate (literal sense).
↳ From the story: Players spent years trying to unravel the Spire's deepest secrets — one clue at a time.
🔬 Science:Researchers are still trying to unravel the mystery of how the brain forms and retrieves memories.
📰 Investigation:The detective spent months unraveling the conspiracy, following a trail of evidence no one else had noticed.
🧶 Literal:Pull one loose thread and the entire sweater will unravel in seconds.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • unravel (v., base form) — untangle or solve. Detectives worked to unravel the conspiracy.
  • unraveled (past, US spelling) — already solved or come apart. The mystery was finally unraveled after 30 years.
  • unravelled (past, UK spelling) — same as above; British English. The plot slowly unravelled.
  • unraveling (present participle) — in the process of coming apart or being solved. The sweater was unraveling at the seam.
  • ravel (v.) — the base verb without un-: means to tangle or become tangled (the opposite sense in literal knitting use). The threads began to ravel.
  • unravel a mystery / conspiracy / plot / story — common collocations. The journalist unraveled the truth behind the scandal.
  • unraveling (n., rare) — used as a noun for a slow coming-apart. The unraveling of the alliance took decades.
  • come apart at the seams (synonym, literal) — fall apart gradually. The plan began to come apart at the seams.
Word 2 of 3
obscure
/əbˈskjʊə/ · əb-SKYOOR · adjective / verb · 模糊的；晦澀的；默默無聞的
Not well known; difficult to understand or discover (adj). Also: to hide or make unclear (v). An obscure secret is one hidden in plain sight, understood only by the most dedicated.
↳ From the story: An obscure event requires you to sacrifice a relic — most players pass it by without knowing what they've missed.
🎵 Music:She collects obscure jazz records from the 1950s — albums so rare even dedicated fans have never heard of them.
📚 Academia:His research focused on an obscure medieval manuscript that only a handful of scholars had ever studied.
🗣️ Language:The contract used such obscure legal jargon that even the lawyers struggled to interpret it.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • obscure (adj., base form) — not well known, or unclear. The origins of the festival are obscure.
  • obscure (v., base form) — to hide or make unclear. Clouds obscured the mountain peak.
  • obscured (adj. / past participle) — hidden from view. The suspect's face was obscured in the security footage.
  • obscuring (present participle) — currently hiding. The fog was obscuring the road ahead.
  • obscurity (n.) — the state of being unknown or unclear. The painter spent years in obscurity before being discovered.
  • obscure + noun — common collocations: obscure reference, obscure rule, obscure fact, obscure origin. The legal text contained an obscure exemption clause.
  • obscure the fact / issue / truth — common verb usage. The report obscured the real reasons for the failure.
  • largely / mostly / relatively obscure — common adverb pairings. The author remained largely obscure until his death.
Word 3 of 3
pivotal
/ˈpɪv.ə.təl/ · PIV-ə-təl · adjective · 關鍵的；樞紐的
Of crucial importance in relation to the development or success of something else; serving as a turning point.
↳ From the story: The answers are pivotal to understanding the game's true meaning — everything hinges on them.
💼 Work:That meeting was pivotal — without the investor's backing, the startup would have folded within a month.
🏛️ History:The invention of the printing press was a pivotal moment in human history, making knowledge accessible to the masses.
🎭 Career:Landing that role was pivotal for her acting career — it opened every door that followed.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • pivotal (adj., base form) — of crucial importance. That was a pivotal decision.
  • pivot (v.) — the base verb: to turn on a central point, or to change direction sharply. The company pivoted from hardware to software.
  • pivot (n.) — the turning point or change in direction. The pivot saved the company.
  • pivoted (past) — already turned or changed direction. She pivoted her career into data science in her thirties.
  • pivoting (present participle) — currently changing direction. The startup is pivoting to enterprise customers.
  • pivotal moment / role / year / decision — very common collocations. The 2008 crash was a pivotal moment for the industry.
  • play a pivotal role in + noun — common verb pattern. She played a pivotal role in the negotiation.
  • pivot on + noun — phrasal verb: depend on / turn on. The whole outcome pivots on this one vote.

💬 Phrases

Phrase 1 of 2
piece together
To gradually assemble disconnected parts or clues to form a complete picture or understanding.
· 拼湊；整理出全貌
↳ From the story: You must piece together clues from relics and events scattered throughout your run.
🔍 Investigation:From the scattered witness statements, the detective slowly pieced together what had happened that night.
📝 Writing:The historian spent a decade piecing together the lost emperor's biography from fragments of letters and official records.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • piece (sth) together — base form. Let's piece together the timeline.
  • pieced (sth) together (past) — already assembled. She pieced together the story from fragments.
  • piecing (sth) together (continuous) — currently assembling. We're piecing together the puzzle.
  • put (sth) together — close variant. He put together a quick demo in 24 hours.
  • piece together + noun — common grammar: piece together evidence, piece together a story, piece together a plan. The archaeologists pieced together the broken mosaic.
  • fit together — related: when individual parts form a coherent whole. The clues finally fit together.
  • reconstruct / reassemble (formal synonyms) — used in academic or technical writing. The team reconstructed the original message from fragments.
  • piece by piece — common adverb phrase: one bit at a time. She rebuilt her confidence piece by piece.
Phrase 2 of 2
against all odds
Despite being extremely unlikely to succeed; in spite of circumstances that made failure almost certain.
· 克服萬難；排除萬難
↳ From the story: Against all odds, you face the Corrupted Heart itself — a battle few ever win.
🏟️ Sport:Against all odds, the underdog team defeated the defending champions in a stunning upset.
❤️ Survival:The climber survived ten days trapped on the mountain against all odds — with no food and temperatures below freezing.
📝 Other forms & aspects
  • against all odds — base form of the idiom. Against all odds, she won.
  • against the odds — common shorter variant. Against the odds, the project shipped on time.
  • despite the odds — similar meaning variant. Despite the odds, they succeeded.
  • odds (n.) — the probability or likelihood. The odds were stacked against us.
  • odds of + gerund — common pattern: odds of winning, odds of success. The odds of survival were less than 5%.
  • overcome the odds — verb form of the same idea. She overcame the odds to become CEO.
  • the odds are against someone — common structure. The odds are against a rookie winning the championship.
  • at great / considerable odds — note: at odds on its own means "in conflict" — a different sense. The two versions are at odds with each other.
