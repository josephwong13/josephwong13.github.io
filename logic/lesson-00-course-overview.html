Course Overview

Scenario: You're reading a political debate. One speaker argues: "If the policy reduces emissions, it is good. The policy reduces emissions. Therefore, it is good." It sounds persuasive — but is it logically watertight, or is something subtly amiss? Formal logic gives you the toolkit to answer precisely, not impressionistically.

What is logic?

DEFINITION

Logic

The study of what makes arguments valid or invalid — independent of subject matter, emotional resonance, or rhetorical polish.[IFL2 Ch1]

Logic is topic-neutral. The same formal structure that validates or invalidates a scientific proof also governs a courtroom argument or a philosophical syllogism. This universality is what makes logic foundational — not domain-specific.

Why formal logic?

Natural language is riddled with ambiguity, vagueness, and concealed assumptions. Formal logic strips an argument down to its skeletal form — discarding rhetorical ornamentation — so we can evaluate its structural integrity with mathematical precision.

KEY INSIGHT

Form determines validity, not content

Validity is a property of form, not substance. Two arguments with the same logical form will stand or fall together — regardless of whether they discuss physics, law, or breakfast cereal.

The logical landscape

COURSE MAP

Three-part journey

Foundations (Module 1): Validity, soundness, logical form — the conceptual vocabulary for the whole course.
Sentence Logic / TFL (Module 2): How connectives (and, or, if, not) create complex propositions. Truth tables. Natural deduction proof systems.
Predicate Logic / QL (Module 3): Quantifiers (all, some), predicates, and identity — a far more expressive logical language.

NOTATION

TFL vs QL vs metalanguage

TFL (Truth-Functional Logic): the formal language of connectives.
QL (Quantified Logic): the formal language of quantifiers and predicates.
Metalanguage: English + symbols (α, β, Γ…) used to talk about TFL/QL expressions.

How these lessons work

FORMAT

Each lesson

One core concept. Bullet-fragment exposition. One worked example with reasoning shown. One self-check quiz (5 questions). Estimated read time: 5 minutes.

⚠ NOVELTY

Symbol-heavy territory

Formal logic introduces new notation — connectives (∧, ∨, →), quantifiers (∀, ∨), turnstiles (⊢, ⊨). These are not decoration. They are precise instruments. Slow down when you encounter a new symbol; make sure you understand what it means before proceeding.
